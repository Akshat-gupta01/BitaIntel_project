VENDOR REGISTRATION, STEP BY STEP

The journey has six registration steps. Step 1 is account signup at
/demo/register; Steps 2-6 are completed in the wizard on
/demo/dashboard/vendor, opened with "Complete Profile Steps (Step 2-6)".

1. CREATE THE ACCOUNT AND PROVIDE CONTACT DETAILS

Where: /demo/register

This step creates the vendor's login and identifies the person authorized to
represent the company. The form asks for:

- Registered company or entity legal name
- Authorized representative's full name and corporate designation
- Official corporate email and mobile number
- Password and password confirmation
- Acceptance of the Terms of Service

The page also presents fields for email and mobile OTPs. In a real registration
system, these would help confirm that the representative controls those contact
channels.

What it means: This creates the basic account, but it does not complete the
company dossier or make the business verified. The account starts in an
unverified state and continues to the vendor dashboard.

2. ADD THE COMPANY PROFILE AND STATUTORY IDENTITY

Where: /demo/dashboard/vendor -> "Complete Profile Steps (Step 2-6)" ->
"Company Profile & GST"

This step establishes the legal identity of the business. It asks for
information such as:

- Legal registered name, trade or brand name, and legal structure
- Incorporation or establishment details
- GSTIN and PAN
- Registered office and other facilities
- Key personnel
- Foundational company documents

Why it matters: These details identify which legal entity is registering and
provide the statutory information that would be checked during compliance
review. The dashboard's progress indicator can move forward as these details
are supplied.

3. CLASSIFY THE BUSINESS AND DESCRIBE ITS CAPABILITIES

Where: The dashboard wizard -> "Classification & Scope"

The vendor describes what the business does by selecting applicable business
types, industry categories, and related subcategories. The form also collects
business activities and operational capabilities. Some capability questions
depend on the classifications selected. Turnover information helps describe the
scale of the business.

Why it matters: Buyers need a consistent way to find vendors by the products,
services, and capabilities they offer. In the current form, the vendor needs to
make the required business-type and category selections to proceed.

4. ADD PRODUCTS, SERVICES, AND QUALITY INFORMATION

Where: The dashboard wizard -> "Products & ISO Standards"

The vendor builds a catalog of what they can supply. Entries can describe
products or services and include relevant details such as specifications,
categories, minimum order quantities, lead times, and pricing information. The
vendor can also record quality certifications, ISO standards, and regulatory
accreditations.

Why it matters: This information helps buyers assess whether the vendor's
offering fits their requirements. The current flow expects at least one catalog
item and certification before proceeding.

5. PROVIDE COMMERCIAL, TRADE, AND BANKING DETAILS

Where: The dashboard wizard -> "Commercial & Banking"

This step covers how the vendor handles business transactions and settlement.
It includes:

- Departmental contacts, such as sales or finance contacts
- Payment terms and supported currencies
- Trade information, including applicable Incoterms
- Bank settlement details, including account holder, bank, account number, and
  IFSC/SWIFT code
- Due-diligence declarations, including sanctions, conflict-of-interest, and
  screening declarations

Why it matters: These details support buyer-vendor communication, transaction
planning, and compliance checks. The form requires key bank information and all
required declarations before allowing the vendor to continue.

6. REVIEW AND SUBMIT THE DOSSIER

Where: The dashboard wizard -> "Review & Submit"

The vendor reviews the information provided across the earlier steps and checks
the statutory document checklist. Before submitting, the vendor must accept
declarations confirming the accuracy of the information, their authority to
submit it, and the required privacy or verification terms.

What happens after submission: The site generates a vendor ID and reference
number, marks the dossier as 100% complete, sets its status to "Verification
Pending", and returns the vendor to the dashboard. 100% complete means
submitted; it does not mean approved.

AFTER SUBMISSION: COMPLIANCE REVIEW

Where: /demo/dashboard/vendor

The dashboard displays the application's review status and reference number.
The interface represents states such as "Verification Pending", "Under
Compliance Review", "Action Required", and "Verified & Active". If verified,
the intended outcome shown by the site is an active profile that buyers can
find.

IMPORTANT DEMO LIMITATION

OTP entry and approval states are simulated. The current site does not send or
validate real OTPs or perform live government or compliance checks. Submission
puts the dossier into a pending state; it does not automatically verify or
approve the vendor.
